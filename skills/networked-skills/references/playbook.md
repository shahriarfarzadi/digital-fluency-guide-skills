# Networked Skills and Leadership Playbook

## Capability map

Classify the need as technical, intellectual, interpersonal, or leadership. Specify behavior, context, practice opportunity, evidence of proficiency, and feedback source. Do not treat a course completion as capability proof.

## Strategic narrative

Build the narrative from shared purpose, company DNA, path to purpose, contribution, relationships, audience mindshifts, distinctive strength, and purpose metrics. The narrative should help people decide and act, not only communicate.

## Exponential brand / ORBIT

Move from push to pull: invite participation, create trust, deliver reciprocal value, and increase relationship gravity over time. Track contribution, retention, trust, and referrals with transactions.

## Networked leadership

Push decisions toward information. Pair autonomy with context, principles, feedback, and escalation. Map the network of relationships rather than relying only on the org chart. Measure connection quality, learning speed, decision latency, and outcomes.

## Decision principles

A useful principle contains intent, boundary, owner, trade-off, and escalation condition. Keep principles short enough to remember and specific enough to change a decision. Review them against actual cases.

## Practice loop

Observe a real decision → name the capability → practice in context → receive feedback → reflect on outcome → update the principle or narrative → repeat.

## Failure modes

- Empowerment without context or accountability.
- Purpose language disconnected from resource allocation.
- Training without a live practice environment.
- Measuring engagement while ignoring decision quality and outcomes.
