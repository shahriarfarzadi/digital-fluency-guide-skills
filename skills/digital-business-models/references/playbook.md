# Digital Business Models Playbook

## Value model map

Map four value sources: things/assets, people/services, ideas/technology/data, and connections/networks. For each participant state the job, contribution, received value, cost, risk, and relationship to the other participants.

| Participant | Contributes | Receives | Pays or captures | Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|

A digital model is more than online distribution. Identify what becomes reusable, data-enhanced, networked, or lower marginal cost.

## Platform test

A platform exists when it enables interactions among at least two participant groups and makes those interactions more valuable through coordination, standards, data, or network effects. State the core interaction, sides, trust mechanism, governance, liquidity problem, and value capture.

## Multi-sided design

Start with the smallest useful interaction. Decide which side is subsidized, what creates repeat participation, how quality is controlled, and how conflicts are resolved. Do not optimize participant count while the core interaction remains weak.

## Business model innovation

1. Draw the current model and its dependencies.
2. Identify a constraint, behavior change, technology shift, or unmet participant need.
3. Generate alternatives that change the value mechanism, not just the feature set.
4. Test the riskiest assumption with a reversible experiment.
5. Update the model using evidence and participant feedback.

## Business model case reading

For every case, separate transferable pattern from context: asset intensity, regulation, distribution, trust, timing, data access, and network density. A successful case is not proof that the same mechanism will work elsewhere.

## Failure modes

- Calling a marketplace a platform without an interaction and governance model.
- Measuring supply or signups instead of successful exchanges.
- Ignoring the weakest participant side.
- Treating a business model canvas as a plan rather than a set of assumptions.
